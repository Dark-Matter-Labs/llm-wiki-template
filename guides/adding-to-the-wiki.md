# Adding to the wiki: what happens to what you put in

## The short answer

Put things in. The wiki checks what arrives. It is a working memory you build on, not a bookmark store.

## What happens when you add something

1. Give Claude the document, recording or notes, and say "add this" or "ingest this".
2. The source is saved as it is. Nobody edits it afterwards.
3. A text source is scanned for hidden text and for instructions aimed at the AI. PDFs are not scanned.
4. Claude writes a short summary page. Every fact cites the source.
5. Claude updates the pages the source touches, and links them both ways.
6. Claude looks for duplicates and contradictions as it files.
7. The automatic checks look for broken links, wording rules and privacy slips.
8. Everything arrives as a proposed change. You say yes before it is saved.

## Why it does not make a mess

- **A duplicate is flagged.** Claude proposes merging the two pages. Nothing merges without a yes.
- **A contradiction is declared, not smoothed over.** A person decides which page stands, or that both stand.
  Disagreement is useful. It shows where people see things differently.
- **What a source says is kept apart from what Claude infers.** Claude marks its own thinking as synthesis.
- **One source is not enough for confidence.** A claim stays at low confidence until other sources agree.

## Why it is not a bookmark store

The wiki keeps summaries and the links between them, not every file. Its answers are good because its pages are
linked and used. A page nobody links to or uses becomes a candidate for retirement. Claude can propose it, and only
a person retires it. Nothing is deleted.

Keep your full archive in your own place, such as Drive. Put in the wiki what you want to build on.

## Who can see it

- A new page takes your wiki's default. In a personal wiki that is private. In a team wiki it is colleagues only.
- Claude asks once when you add something. You can change a page's tier later.
- Contacts, leads and money figures stay private. They never travel to a team wiki.

## A good way to start

1. Pick one large topic you hold.
2. Give Claude everything you have on it.
3. Ask for one page on that topic.
4. Read the contradictions it finds across your own files.
5. Repeat for the next topic.

## A job for you, or for the model?

Give the model the reading, sorting and summarising. Keep the decisions for people: what stands, what is true and
what you commit to.
