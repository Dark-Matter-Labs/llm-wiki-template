# Asking the wiki

Talk to Claude in plain words. These phrases start each kind of help.

| Say | What you get |
|---|---|
| "What does the wiki say about X?" | An answer drawn from your pages, with the source of every claim. |
| "Add this" or "ingest this" | A new source filed, as in [Adding to the wiki](adding-to-the-wiki.md). |
| "What's waiting?" | What needs you: proposals, decisions and work nobody put forward. |
| "Catch me up" | A short briefing on what changed. |
| "Check the wiki" | A health check: broken links, stale pages, duplicates and gaps. |
| "I have a new idea" | A thinking partner that pushes back and connects it to what the wiki holds. |
| "What don't we know?" | The open questions the wiki holds, and what would answer them. |
| "Share this with the team" | The page proposed to your team wiki, checked for privacy first. |

## Reading an answer

- **Every claim cites its source.** Something like `(raw/report.pdf)` after a sentence names the document it came
  from. Ask Claude to show you the passage.
- **Synthesis is marked.** When Claude connects things on its own, it says so.
- **Confidence** says how well a page is supported: low means one source, high means several agree.
- **Validation** says who stood behind a page. "machine" means nobody has yet. A person's name means they did.
- **A disagreement is shown, not hidden.** Two pages can disagree. A person decides which stands, or that both stand.

## When the wiki does not know

A good answer says so plainly. If the wiki holds nothing on a question, Claude tells you, and suggests what source
would answer it. It does not fill the gap with guesses.

## Questions it will not answer

The wiki answers questions about knowledge. It declines to track people, for example who has been doing what, or
when someone works.
