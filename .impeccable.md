# Impeccable — project design context

Design context for the **published web pages** in `docs/` (the xCO / Dark Matter Labs
editorial system). Written 2026-08-07. Read this before any design work here; the
`publish-web` skill references it.

## Design Context

### Users

Published pages are **sent as direct links** — in email, Slack, WhatsApp — never browsed
from an index (`docs/index.html` is deliberately a minimal `noindex` landing that must
never catalogue pages). The link preview is therefore the first impression, and more
people see it than open the page.

Two primary audiences, both of whom arrive cold and owe the page no patience:

- **Funders and institutional partners** — deciding whether to back or partner on an
  instrument. They need credibility, rigour, provenance, and a legible ask. They will
  judge seriousness from the page's craft before they judge the argument.
- **Peers and practitioners** — people working in systemic risk, civic finance and
  climate adaptation. They share the vocabulary and want the mechanism and the argument,
  not an explainer.

Both read on desktop *and* on phones, often forwarded mid-thread. Neither is a consumer
audience; neither wants to be marketed at.

The job to be done: **understand a serious argument quickly enough to decide whether to
engage with it.**

### Brand Personality

**Rigorous · editorial · unhedged.**

The register is a research institute's working paper, not a startup landing page. It
should feel like something written by people who have thought about this for years and
are not trying to sell you anything — confidence carried by precision, not by emphasis.

Emotional goal: **considered seriousness.** A reader should feel they are being levelled
with, including about what is uncertain. Flagged tensions and caveats are carried
through, never smoothed away — honesty is part of the brand, not a disclaimer.

### Aesthetic Direction

The **xCO design system** (`docs/assets/xco.css`, derived from the xCO style guide and
shared with other Dark Matter Labs work). Editorial, typographic, structural. Two
registers, both in active use:

- **Light** (`xco.css`) — concept papers, essays, summaries, process notes.
- **Dark "Dusk Tritone"** (`xco-dusk.css`) — instruments, atlases, prospectuses, decks.

Choosing between them is a documented rule in `publish-web`, not a per-page whim.

**Fixed (brand identity — do not change):**
- Palette hues: navy `--shadow` (OKLCH H 263°), orange `--midtone` (H 48°). One accent.
- Typefaces: Crimson Pro (prose) · Inter (statements) · DM Mono (structure/labels).
- The golden-ratio grid (61.8 / 38.2) and the Fibonacci spacing scale (13/21/34/55/89).

**Anti-references:** SaaS landing pages, consultancy decks, "thought leadership" blog
templates. Specifically: no glassmorphism, no gradient text, no neon-on-dark, no
icon-above-every-heading, no hero-metric template, no rounded-rectangle-with-drop-shadow.
The system currently uses none of these and must continue not to.

### Design Principles

1. **Design is how the argument becomes legible.** Never decoration. Every typographic
   and spatial choice either helps the reader follow the reasoning or is removed.
2. **Structure by role, not by look.** Serif argues, sans states, mono labels. Spacing
   encodes relationship (each Fibonacci step = one level of proximity). A choice that
   can't be justified by role is taste, and taste is not a reason here.
3. **The accent is punctuation.** Orange marks structure, never fills space. Past roughly
   5% of the page it stops meaning anything. Rarity is the whole mechanism.
4. **Honest before polished.** Carry the caveats, the contested status, the confidence
   level. A page that oversells is off-brand even if it looks good.
5. **Accessible is not optional.** **WCAG 2.2 AA** is a shipping gate, not an aspiration:
   4.5:1 body contrast, visible focus, a working skip link, real landmarks, reduced-motion
   respected, 24px targets. Institutional readers include public-sector users on locked-down
   browsers — so progressive enhancement, never a hard dependency on new CSS.

## House exceptions to `impeccable:frontend-design`

The frontend-design skill is applied here as a **craft layer inside the brand**. These
deviations from its defaults are deliberate and reasoned — not oversights. Anything not
listed here, the skill wins.

| Skill says | We do | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Don't use Inter (overused) | Inter for headings/statements | It is the xCO brand face, shared across Dark Matter Labs work. Swapping it is not a design improvement, it is a brand break. |
| Don't use mono as "technical" shorthand | DM Mono for eyebrows, labels, section kickers | The warning targets mono-as-developer-cosplay. Here it is an *editorial* device — a third voice marking structure against serif prose. Never used for reading text. |
| Don't use thick one-side accent borders | `.quote-block` and `.accent-card` keep a left rule | The skill's target is the rounded-card-with-colored-edge cliché. These are square, structural, and follow a print convention (the marginal rule). Reviewed and kept — see below for the card case. |
| Prefer OKLCH | Hex values, with OKLCH derivations recorded | Palette is three colours that rarely change, and pages must render on old institutional browsers. Where OKLCH is used it ships **with a hex fallback first**, never as a hard dependency. |

**Adopted from the skill (changes made 2026-08-07):**
- **Tinted neutral.** Paper moved off pure `#FFFFFF` to `#FBFCFE` — OKLCH
  `99.1% 0.0025 264`, carrying a trace of the brand navy hue. Pure white "never appears in
  nature"; the tint creates subconscious cohesion at a chroma too small to read as a colour.
  *Second-order effect caught and fixed:* this lowered `--midtone-ink` from 4.55:1 to
  4.44:1 (failing AA), so the ink was re-derived to `#C34F0D` at 4.60:1 on the new paper.
- **Card monotony.** A fixed 2-up grid repeated down a page is the skill's "identical card
  grids" failure. `.aside-grid` keeps the 2-up default but gains `--wide` and `--auto`
  variants so a page can vary its rhythm.
- **The AI-slop test** is now a required step in the `publish-web` verification loop.
