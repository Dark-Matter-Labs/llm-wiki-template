# Start here

Welcome. This is **your** wiki, and you can use it without any technical know-how. You won't need a
terminal, you won't need to learn git, and you won't need anyone to walk you through it.

If something on this page confuses you, that's our mistake, not yours. Tell us where you got stuck, and
we'll fix the page.

It takes about an hour, and most of that is reading.

*Want the technical version? It's in `TECHNICAL.md`. You don't need it to use the wiki.*

---

## What this is

Think of it as a **notebook that organises itself**. You bring things to it: reports, notes, ideas,
questions. Claude reads them, writes short summaries, connects them to what's already there, and keeps it
all tidy. The more you add, the more useful it gets.

The whole thing works like a conversation:

1. **You say what you want**, in plain English.
2. **Claude does the work** and tells you what it found or changed.
3. **You say yes** before anything is kept.

Nothing goes into your wiki without you saying yes.

---

## What you need

Just two things:

1. **A GitHub account** that can open this wiki. If you can see this page, you have one.
2. **Claude**, at **[claude.ai/code](https://claude.ai/code)**. Open it in your browser, pick this wiki,
   and start typing. There's nothing to install.

## Your first ten minutes

Open [claude.ai/code](https://claude.ai/code), pick this wiki, and try one of these:

> *"Catch me up."*
> *"What does this wiki say about \<something you care about\>?"*
> *"What's in here?"*

Read the answer. Notice two things. Every fact says where it came from. And anything Claude worked out
for itself is marked as its own thinking, not passed off as fact. That's the heart of how the wiki works.

That's it. You've used it. Everything below is detail.

## Adding your first document

Pick something of yours that isn't in the wiki yet: a paper, some meeting notes, a deck, a note of an
idea. Then either:

- **Paste it** into the chat and say *"please add this to the wiki."* This works for most things.
- **Give Claude the link**, if it's a web page.
- **For a PDF or Word file**, say *"I'd like to add a PDF, how do I get it to you?"* Claude will walk
  you through it, one step at a time.

Claude will read it and tell you the main points. It will ask you **who should be able to see it**
(more on that just below; if you're not sure, say "keep it private"). Then it writes a summary and links
it to everything related.

When it's done, Claude tells you in plain words what it learned, and asks ***"Shall I merge this?"***
That's the wiki's way of saying *"shall I save this?"* **Say yes**, and it's saved. **Say no**, or say
nothing, and it waits safely for later. Nothing is lost.

Then ask *"What does the wiki now say about \<your topic\>?"* and watch your document show up in the
answer.

## Who can see what

Every page has a label that says **how far it's allowed to travel**. There are four:

| Label | In plain words |
| --- | --- |
| **`private`** | **Just for you.** It never leaves your wiki. **Every new page starts here.** |
| **`internal`** | **For colleagues.** It can be shared with your team, but never goes on the public internet. |
| **`unlisted`** | **Anyone with the link.** It can go on a website, but it's hidden, not listed or searchable. |
| **`public`** | **Anyone.** It can go on the public internet. |

To change a label, just say so: *"make this page internal."* Claude won't change a label unless you ask.

Nothing is shared with your team unless you ask for it, and even then another team member checks it
first.

**One honest caveat.** A label controls where a page can *travel*. It doesn't lock the wiki itself. The
people who can open your wiki, including Dark Matter Labs' GitHub admins, can read every page in it.
If something must stay hidden even from them, don't put it here.

For the full picture in plain words, read **`HOW-IT-WORKS.md`**.

## Things you can ask for

You never need to learn commands. Just say what you want. Some examples:

| Say something like… | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| "Add this report" | Claude reads it, summarises it, and links it to everything related |
| "What does the wiki say about…?" | An answer, with every fact showing where it came from |
| "Catch me up" | A short summary of what's new |
| "Check the wiki" | A health check: what's out of date, what disagrees, what's missing |
| "Help me think about this idea" | Claude asks you hard questions before you commit to something |
| "Challenge this" | Claude makes the strongest case *against* a position |
| "Share this with the team" | A careful copy goes to the team's wiki, for someone there to check |
| "What's waiting for me?" | A list of anything you started and didn't finish |
| "Is this ready to publish?" | A check of sources and labels before anything goes out |

Not sure what's possible? Ask *"what can you do?"*

## Fixing small things yourself

You don't need Claude to fix a typo. **`EDITING.md`** shows you how: click the pencil icon on a page,
make the change, and save it.

A simple rule for when to do it yourself: if the fix touches **one page**, it's yours. If it touches
**many pages**, like renaming something that other pages mention, ask Claude.

---

## Why you can't break it

- **Every change gets checked automatically** before it's saved: spelling, broken links and missing
  details.
- **Every change can be undone** on its own.
- **Nothing is ever deleted to tidy up.** Old pages are marked as retired, and they stay readable.
- **Your original documents are never edited.** A report that said something stays the record of what it
  said.

If a check complains, ask Claude what it means. Nothing is lost either way.

---

## Two things only you can do

**Standing behind a page.** When you've read a page and you're happy to put your name to it, that's
worth recording. Claude will sometimes suggest a page for you to check, but it can never confirm one on
your behalf. Confirming is a person's job. Say *"what should I stand behind?"* to get a few suggestions.
To confirm one, you add three short lines to the top of the page with your name and the date. Claude can
show you exactly where, and `TECHNICAL.md` has the lines to copy.

**Settling a disagreement.** Sometimes two pages say different things. Claude will point it out and stop
there, because deciding which one is right is your call, not a computer's.
