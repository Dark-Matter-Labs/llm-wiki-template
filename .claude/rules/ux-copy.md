# Titles, headings, labels and short text

Given by Prateek on 24 November 2026, from his edits to the Seascaping BANOS page, the brain trust reading pack and the
xCO deck. Gurden added them to every wiki the same day. The ten rules are Prateek's, word for word. His before-and-after
examples and his checklist are in `raw/decisions/ux-copy-rules-prateek-2026-11-24.md`.

## The rules

1. Titles, headings and labels are literal and direct. Start with the words people scan for.
2. Use sentence case. Put no full stop on a heading.
3. Do not write narrative or question headings, such as "How the model works", "What it is not yet" or "Who does what".
4. An update's heading states the update itself, not its topic.
5. A button names what it opens, in a noun or a short verb phrase. Never "Open" or "Click here" alone.
6. A description says what the thing is, in plain words. It does not repeat the heading.
7. Remove a subtitle, label or note that adds no fact.
8. Give one date per item: the date it is finished, not the date of its next step.
9. Use the agreed name for a thing every time. In a sentence, write a common noun in lower case: "the cascading risk model".
10. Check every title, heading and label against these rules before you show a page, slide or deck.

## How they apply here (Gurden, 24 November 2026)

- **Where.** Wiki pages, published pages, slides, decks and on-screen labels. Not skill files, rule files or other
  instructions for Claude.
- **A question stays a question.** Where the thing itself is a question, its title is the question: a learning
  question, an inquiry page or an open question. Rule 3 covers headings that describe something else.
- **From now on.** Apply them to new pages, and to a page when you change it for another reason. Never rewrite old
  headings only to meet them.
