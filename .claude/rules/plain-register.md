# Plain register, and suggested changes

Given by Gurden on 6 October 2026 for every wiki. The two rule sets below are his, word for word.
The notes after them say how they sit beside the other writing rules here.

## The rules

PLAIN REGISTER (Simplified Technical English, by licence):
- Wiki pages are STE-FLAVOURED unless a section is a procedure or instruction. Those sections are STRICT.
- Decide the licence first. Decision text, instructions, UI copy and agent prompts are STRICT. Explanations, wiki pages and changelogs are STE-FLAVOURED. Encounter writing is EXEMPT.
- STRICT: 20 words or fewer per sentence. One instruction per sentence. Active voice. Use one word for one action every time.
- STE-FLAVOURED: 25 words or fewer per sentence. Active voice unless the actor is unknown. Vocabulary may vary.
- Both: no semicolons. No phrasal verbs (start, not kick off). Verbs, not nominalizations. Noun clusters of three words or fewer. Use a list for three or more steps. One topic per paragraph.
- Keep every hedge. Never change "may" to "is". Never add a fact, cause or number the source did not state.
- Keep a necessary term and define it once.

SUGGESTED CHANGES ARE PROVISIONAL:
- Do not overwrite the current wording. Show your change as a provisional block at its target.
- Use this form: "> **[PROVISIONAL / proposed]** <id> · <target> · proposed by <agent or person> · <date>", then "~~current~~ → **proposed**", then "Why:" with one reason.
- Keep the current wording visible. Do not delete it.
- Do not mark a change adopted. Only a named person adopts a change.

## How they sit beside the other rules

The licences are the ones the Polyphonic Communication Style Guide already names: Encounter,
Explanation and Decision. A wiki page is an Explanation, so it is STE-FLAVOURED by default.

`ai-detox` still governs the words and shapes it bans. Its "under 20 words most of the time" and
the limits above agree. Where a list is needed for three or more steps, use one. `ai-detox` bars
lists from running prose, not from procedures.

`talking.md` governs replies to people in a session. This rule governs written text: pages,
instructions, prompts and interface copy.

Quotations stay as written. A source's own sentence keeps its length, its semicolons and its
phrasal verbs, the same reason `raw/` is never edited.

## Where suggested changes apply

They apply to wording a person wrote or has stood behind. That covers four cases:

1. a page at `validation: self`, `peer` or `collective`
2. a person's own draft or document
3. a style guide
4. any text where a person has asked to see changes as suggestions

There, a change appears as a provisional block beside the current wording. Only a named person
adopts it.

They do not stop routine upkeep. When a source arrives, the ingest still updates the pages it
touches. That work already waits for a person, because every change goes out as a proposed change
that someone approves before it is saved.

This scope is a reading of the rule, noted on 6 October 2026. If the rule is meant more widely,
widen it here.
