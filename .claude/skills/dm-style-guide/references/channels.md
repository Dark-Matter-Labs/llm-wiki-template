# Choosing the right channel and audience (Section 5)

Where a piece is published shapes how it should be written — each channel reaches
a different audience with a different level of attention. Match the writing to
the channel *before* posting. Primary channels carry substantive thinking;
secondary channels extend reach and are largely visual or repost-driven.

| Channel | Audience & attention | What to write for it |
|---|---|---|
| **Medium** (Provocations) | Peers across the systems-change ecosystem; researchers, practitioners, curious public. Willing to read at length if rewarded. | Long-form provocations and propositions. Natural home for the context → proposition → action arc, coined terms (properly justified), and a full Lineage section. After posting, add to the Dm Provocations publication. |
| **Substack** | A subscribed audience opted in to follow Dm's ongoing thinking; expects a thread over time. | Narrative, 'work-out-loud' reflections and series connecting threads across projects. Slightly more personal and serial than Medium. Current practice: publish simultaneously on Medium and Substack. |
| **LinkedIn** | Professional network — partners, funders, peers, prospective collaborators. Low attention; scanning. | Long-form text capped at 3,000 characters. Lead with the point. Better to attach a PDF (or images) carrying the detail than cram it into the post. Tag partners and relevant people in the post or first comment. Often used to spread long-form articles as attached PDFs. |
| **Instagram** | Broad, visual-first public; very low text attention. | Visual-first: posts for major updates, stories for minor ones and sharing links. Lead with the image; keep words minimal. |

## One channel, many audiences — a real risk to manage
Historically almost all writing went out through one channel, so a piece written
for one audience is read by another. This has caused trust issues with partners —
an exploratory provocation read as a settled position, or internal-facing framing
seen by a client.

Two safeguards:
1. **State the piece's status and intended reader near the top** ('this is an
   early provocation, not a Dm position' — see the Publishing Guide's framing
   note).
2. **Write each version for its channel.** A piece often lives across several: a
   full provocation on Medium, a narrative framing on Substack, a
   3,000-character summary with a PDF on LinkedIn, a visual on Instagram. The
   argument is the same; the writing is not. Do not paste one text into all four
   before thinking about the strategy.
