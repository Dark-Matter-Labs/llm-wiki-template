# Attribution Guide

How Dm credits intellectual authorship. Not a gatekeeping mechanism — it makes
visible the contributions too often left invisible. The question it asks is not
'do I have to?' but 'am I being honest about where this came from?'

Anyone can ask 'how should we credit this?', say 'my contribution is missing',
or pause publication until attribution is resolved. Route questions to
#help-publishing (or #help-design for visual-source questions).

## Step 1 — Map your contributors {#step-1}

Name everyone who contributed and in what capacity, across all contribution
types — not just text. Visual designers and coders are authors too.

| Type | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Foundational | Originated the core framework, concept, or analytical pathway | Developed the theoretical model this builds on; wrote the original framing |
| Condition building | Conceived the work, built partnerships, secured/provided funding | Scoping, fundraising, partnership development, internal championing |
| Adaptation & contextualisation | Built upon, adapted, or contextualised existing work | Applied a framework to a new context; developed existing content further |
| Production | Produced the output | Writing, design, illustration, data visualisation, software development |
| Feedback | Reviewed, critiqued, advised | Peer reviewer, editor, strategic advisor |

**Conceptual contributors** who build ideas others later implement are often the
least visible — name them even if they were not involved in finalising the
output.

**Background IP.** Work, methods, or ideas a colleague brings into Dm from
outside remain their IP. If your publication builds on someone's background IP,
attribution *and* consent are both required. This is a relational obligation,
not a formality.

**External sources.** Credit any framework, model, or concept that did not
originate at Dm (e.g. an iceberg model). Do not present it as Dm's own unless Dm
has substantially transformed it. When in doubt, link to the original.

**Licensing** (Dm has no single IP model — the right approach serves public
benefit):
- Most publicly published work defaults to **CC BY-SA 4.0** (Attribution-
  ShareAlike): copy, redistribute, remix, build upon, even commercially, with
  credit and share-alike.
- Where commercial use should be restricted: **CC BY-NC 4.0**.
- Some outputs may use structured licensing or a dedicated vehicle to enable
  uptake at speed/scale — a legitimate choice, not a contradiction of values.
- Unsure (especially funded-project or partnership work with its own IP terms)?
  Ask on #help-publishing and refer to the Publishing Guide.

**Consent practices.** Before publishing, tell contributors how you intend to
credit them and invite corrections. Draft the credit block first and share it
('Here is how I'm planning to credit you — does this work?') rather than asking
the open-ended 'how would you like to be credited?'. Not everyone wants to be
credited — respect that; credit is not something to impose. If a contributor has
left Dm, their contributions remain — attribute them using the role they held at
the time.

## Step 2 — Decide how to credit {#step-2}

### The credit block (at the end of the document)
Adapt to output type, but aim to include:
```
Written by: [Name], [Name] — primary authors
Design and visuals by: [Name]
Code and development by: [Name]
Conceptual development by: [Name]
Conditions built by: [Name]
Reviewed and edited by: [Name]
```
Declare AI use if AI was used in any phase, e.g.:
> Parts of this document were drafted with the assistance of AI tools like
> Claude and Midjourney; the underlying research, values, and judgements remain
> those of the human contributors named in this lineage.

### Lineage & version history
Acknowledge the historical work this builds on, prior versions, and the
'working out loud' stance. Include: what it draws on (with authors + year),
the project context, the wider ecosystem of contributors, the licence, and an
invitation to add missing contributions ('If you know of related work or a
contribution missing from this lineage, we welcome additions.'). Link to the
website's fuller statement on lineage and intellectual responsibility.

### In-text citation
- Hyperlink to originals wherever possible — open knowledge is only meaningful
  if people can find the source.
- Unpublished foundational work: describe contextually ('drawing on internal
  research by [Full Name], [Year]') and link to any accessible version; if none,
  note status clearly ('unpublished internal document, [Year]').
- Relational citation: credit important conversations, messages, and
  presentations that directly shaped the argument, even if internal or unwritten.
- Academic-style outputs: pick a citation style and be consistent — consistency
  matters more than which system.

### Visual and diagrammatic work
- Substantially modified: credit the most recent author and note lineage
  ('Adapted from [original author], [year]').
- Minor modification: retain original credit, add 'Modified by [name], [year]'.
- Used unchanged: credit the original author as is.
- Unsure of source: ask in #help-design or ping @visual-designers.

### Platform-specific notes
- **Medium / Substack**: credit block at the bottom; in-text citation,
  hyperlinks, footnotes as needed.
- **Client reports/deliverables**: credit block on cover/back or colophon;
  check whether the client agreement permits named individual attribution or
  requires organisational authorship only.
- **Grant proposals**: follow funder formatting; include a credit block in any
  accompanying materials.
- **Websites**: see Appendix A.

## Appendix A — Websites and other digital outputs {#appendix-a}

Full principles apply. Before going live:
- **Attribution**: name writers, designers, developers (often the first casualty
  of a deadline). Departed contributors stay on project pages — distinguish
  'current team' vs 'original contributors' if needed, but do not erase history.
- **Clarity of narrative and audience**: does the site say what the project is,
  why it matters, and who it's for? Circulate to at least one person outside the
  team before launch.
- **Accessibility (not optional)**: works on mobile; all images have alt text;
  colour contrast meets WCAG 2.1 AA (verify with WebAIM Contrast Checker). These
  are minimums, not aspirations.
- **Ecosystem connection**: link to darkmatterlabs.org and related projects
  where appropriate. Low DML connectivity is a valid strategic choice if it is
  conscious and documented, not an oversight.
- **Technical stewardship**: a named person must know how the site works and can
  maintain it.
