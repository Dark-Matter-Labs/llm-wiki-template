# AI Usage Guide

The entry point across the other guides — it covers the process *before*
attribution (which handles AI declaration in the credit block), publishing
(platform choice and signoff), and style (output quality). Not about *whether*
to use AI, but using it safely, honestly, and consistently with Dm's quality.

## Before you use AI {#before}

### 1. Classify the sensitivity of the material
Not everything should go into an AI tool. The principle is **minimum necessary
data** — upload an extract or anonymised summary where that is sufficient; do
not share a full dataset when a few rows will do.

| Class | Rule |
|---|---|
| Public or published | Generally fine to use |
| Internal working material | Use with care; consider what is strictly necessary |
| Confidential (partner/client docs, commercially sensitive, legal/contractual, financial, restricted-licence data) | Do not upload without explicit permission |
| Personal or identifiable (raw interview transcripts, participant data, personal stories, community research) | Do not upload unless participants were informed AI-supported processing may occur and consented; even then, anonymise |
| Sensitive personal data | Do not upload |

### 2. Check what the tool actually has access to
Connected tools (Slack, Notion, Drive, project folders) may have broader access
than intended. Before connecting, confirm: what it can access and whether access
is opt-in/opt-out; whether sensitive folders can be excluded; whether prompts
and outputs are retained by the provider; whether the provider uses data for
training; whether admin controls exist. **Privacy before convenience** — if you
cannot confirm these, do not connect the tool.

## Using AI well {#using-well}
AI works best with a clearly bounded task, a defined evidence base, and an
explicit output format.
- **Define the task first**: purpose, audience, and what decisions/actions it
  supports. Vague prompts produce vague outputs.
- **Build a controlled source base**: give a defined set of documents rather
  than open-ended research questions. AI drawing only on training data may
  produce irrelevant, outdated, or fabricated outputs.
- **Separate planning from execution**: for complex tasks, use AI first to
  develop a research plan (sub-questions, evidence needs, output format,
  assumptions to test), review it, then produce content.
- **Request structured outputs** (tables, comparison grids, evidence matrices,
  argument outlines): easier to check than prose and surface gaps more visibly.
- **Do not let AI set the base prompt without oversight**: where possible, set a
  system prompt that flags uncertainty, avoids unverified claims, and does not
  optimise for sounding confident. Fluency is not accuracy.

## Reviewing and validating AI-assisted work {#reviewing}
AI-generated material should not leave your desk without human review — this is
the point at which the work becomes yours.

**Check all citations, figures, and factual claims.** AI can produce references
that look authoritative but are fabricated, misattributed, or subtly wrong. For
any statistic, citation, legal claim, financial figure, or policy reference:
confirm the source exists; confirm it says what the AI claims; check figures for
the right geography, year, and category; check claims are not out of context;
ask whether a more authoritative source exists. Especially important for
external or partner/funder/public work.

**Check for flattened complexity** (the same check the Style Guide adopts):
- Has uncertainty been removed or softened?
- Has disagreement between sources been smoothed over?
- Have minority or critical perspectives been dropped?
- Has a small number of sources been generalised beyond what they support?
- If the output is tidier than the reality it describes, that is a problem.

**Check that the argument is yours.** AI can produce well-structured text that
does not reflect your position or the specific context. Read as a critical
editor, not a recipient, and revise.

**Apply a higher review threshold for**: published external outputs; policy/
strategy documents that could inform critical decisions; work involving
community research or participant voices; anything attributed to Dm as an
organisation.

## Before you publish (AI-specific) {#before-publish}
In addition to the Attribution and Publishing checks:
- [ ] All factual claims, citations, and figures verified against primary sources
- [ ] No confidential, sensitive, or non-consented material was used
- [ ] Reviewed by a human for accuracy, complexity, and argument — not just polish
- [ ] AI usage declared in the credit block (per the Attribution Guide)
- [ ] If participants/partners contributed material, they were informed of AI use
- [ ] If images were AI-generated, this is stated and licensing checked
