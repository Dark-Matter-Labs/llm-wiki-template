# Source proportion — the analysis must not outgrow its source

The failure this prevents is not inaccuracy. It is a mapping table longer than the section it maps, a
paraphrase standing where the source's own words should be, and an inference sitting in the author's
voice on a page going out under someone else's name.

Indy's correction, 2026-08-09: *"can we please not extrapolate stuff…"* — and the diagnostic written
down the same day, which is the whole of this skill: **volume of synthesis relative to the source is
the signal.** When the analysis layer starts exceeding the thing being analysed, stop and ask.
Verified as a pattern by Gurden, 2026-08-13.

## Method

1. **Locate the boundary.** For each analytical block, name the source section it interprets. A block
   that cannot name its section is not interpretation — it is new claim-making, and belongs on a page
   of the wiki's own, not on a page about the source.

2. **Measure.** Compare the length of the analysis against that section. Longer is a stop signal, not
   a style preference. The ratio is a heuristic, not a limit — but crossing 1:1 means the burden of
   proof has moved, and the block has to earn its length out loud.

3. **Check the voice, separately.** Three specific failures, all seen on one page:
   - **Commentary in the author's document.** Anything the wiki thinks, on a page published under
     Dark Matter Labs' name, read by a stranger as the author's own view. Remove it or attribute it.
   - **Paraphrase standing in for a section.** If the source has a §9, the page carries §9 — not the
     wiki's summary of §9 wearing its heading.
   - **Inference dressed as footing.** "Several already have footing in existing xCO work" is an
     inference; "Illustrative expressions, not commitments" is the note's own framing. Prefer the
     source's framing every time it exists.

4. **Mark what survives.** Interpretation that earns its place stays, labelled — *(this wiki's
   reading:)*, "synthesis", or an explicit section of its own. Labelled inference is publishable;
   unlabelled inference is not.

5. **When in doubt, cut and offer.** Removing an over-read costs one sentence in chat ("I cut X — say
   the word and it goes back"). Shipping one costs a correction on a published page.

## Output

Either a clean pass — the blocks named, the proportions stated — or the specific cuts, each with the
section it exceeded and where its content went (removed, relabelled, or moved to a wiki page of its
own). Never a silent trim.

## Rules
- **The source's own words beat the wiki's better version of them.** Always, on a page about the source.
- Length is the tell, not the offence. A long analysis that is clearly the wiki's own, on the wiki's
  own page, is fine — the offence is length *borrowing the source's authority*.
- This does not license thinness. Under-reading a source is a different failure and this skill has
  nothing to say about it; that is what `office-hours` and `joker` are for.
- Applies hardest to `publish-web` and `contribute` — anything leaving the repo — and to `ingest`,
  where the over-read gets written down as though it were the record.
