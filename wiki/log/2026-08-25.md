## [2026-08-25] rebuild | Retrieval and lifecycle, carried across the federation

Two findings from indy-llm-wiki, applied here.

**Routing must cost a constant, not a proportion.** There, `index.md` had grown to ~55k tokens and
was read before every operation, against a corpus whose median page is ~1.5k — the map had grown as
fast as the territory. `tools/search.py` now ranks **title, tags and description above body text**
and excludes catalogues, so a ranked shortlist costs ~1k instead of a scan. The `query` skill routes
through it: **search, then graph, then read** — and never loads pages in order to decide which pages
to load. Body-only TF-IDF was why the tool existed for months and nothing used it: long essays
outranked the concept page a question was about.

**A staleness clock a migration cannot reset.** `tools/staleness.py` reports when a page's *body*
last changed, ignoring frontmatter-only commits. Git mtime cannot be trusted for this: one schema
backfill rewrites every page's frontmatter and resets every clock at once — in indy's wiki that made
766 pages all report the same 10 days.

**Dormancy.** `status: dormant` is now in the schema as a weight-and-visibility change, never a
deletion: the file stays readable, its row moves to an attic shelf, and `compute_gravity.py` stops
counting it so retired thinking cannot keep pulling the corpus's centre. Only a human sets it; `lint`
proposes and never applies. The lint pass also reports repo history alongside the count and declines
to present a list when history is under twice the threshold — in a young wiki "untouched 42 days"
means "arrived in the first bulk ingest", which is youth, not death.

Gravity now also skips `wiki/index/`, so the map cannot vote on where the territory's centre is.
